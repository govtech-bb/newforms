Find your polyclinic

Barbados is divided into catchment areas, and each area is served by one polyclinic. Find out which polyclinic serves the area where you live, what services it offers, and when its clinics run. The one nearest you is not always the one assigned to you.

Last updated on September 1st, 2026

      About the list. Each polyclinic serves the parishes shown in the matching colour on the map. Select any facility for its services, opening hours and clinic days.       Reading the map. Solid areas are parishes served by one polyclinic. A dashed edge means the parish is shared with another polyclinic. A dotted outline marks a parish that also has its own outpatient clinic, open on set days only, with the days shown on the pin. Round pins are polyclinics; square pins are outpatient clinics. Edgar Cochrane Polyclinic is a pin with no area, because no catchment is published for it.

      About these areas. Each polyclinic publishes its catchment as a list of parishes, so the map colours whole parishes. Four parishes — St. James, St. Thomas, St. Philip and St. Michael — are shared between polyclinics. The split inside St. Michael is traced by hand because no source publishes it. Areas may change; this map was last updated on September 1st, 2026.

      If you live near the edge of a parish, confirm which polyclinic serves you by calling the Ministry of Health and Wellness on (246) 536-3800 before travelling.

      Sources and known data problems

      Sources are graded, because they are not equally trustworthy. Anything marked unconfirmed on this page came only from Tier D.

      Tier A — official, authoritative

      • health.gov.bb — the nine polyclinic names, addresses and 536-xxxx phone numbers; "all polyclinics 08:30–16:30, Monday to Friday"; Winston Scott 24/7; the island-wide list of services; and the St. Joseph and St. Andrew outpatient clinics.

      Tier B — official but old

      • Ministry of Health Polyclinic Procedure Manual (2011) — the 40-service list, the programmes required at every polyclinic, and the asthma treatment room and nebulisation protocol. Fifteen years old; the extended hours in it predate the 2019 24-hour launch.
      • Barbados Government Information Service (2019) — 24-hour service at Winston Scott and David Thompson, the urgent-care definition, and pharmacy hours.

      Tier C — independent press

      • Barbados Today, 1 May 2026 — the St. Thomas Outpatient Clinic reopening on 5 May 2026 and its Tuesday/Thursday/Friday general-practice days.

      Tier D — not an official source, must be confirmed

      The individual polyclinic websites on polyclinics.wixsite.com are not a government source. Checked 1 September 2026: they are not linked from health.gov.bb, from health.gov.bb's homepage, or from gov.bb; they sit on a free wixsite.com subdomain; they serve a Wix free-tier advertising banner; they have no Wayback Machine snapshots at all; they carry pre-migration phone numbers; and five of the nine publish the wrong catchment paragraph. Authorship is unknown.

      Everything in the following list came only from those pages and must be confirmed with the Ministry before beta: the per-polyclinic service list, every specialist clinic day, Winston Scott's eye / renal / STI / chest / non-communicable-disease clinic detail, the urgent-care closing times, the twelve environmental-health functions, the satellite weekly timetables, the founding dates, and the catchment descriptions this map's parish colouring is built from.

      It is kept on the page because there is no alternative — health.gov.bb publishes no clinic days at all — but it is labelled everywhere it appears rather than shown as fact.

      What is still approximate or unverified

      • Parish boundaries come from geoBoundaries (gbOpen BRB ADM1, full resolution), licensed CC BY 2.5, source data geoBoundaries / Wikimedia, last updated January 2023. This is open data, not the Barbados Lands & Surveys official boundary set, and it can disagree with an official address near a border: it places our approximate pin for Warrens in St. Thomas, where the Ministry gives Eunice Gibson Polyclinic's address as Warrens, St. Michael. Replace with the official boundary set before beta.
      • It is not established that a single set of catchment boundaries governs clinical care. The official dataset (a Lands & Surveys layer named "Polyclinic_Catchment_Areas", 8 polygons covering the whole island) has only ever been validated for routing Environmental Health Department applications to a polyclinic's environmental health mailbox. Environmental health districts and public health nursing catchments are run by different departments and need not share boundaries. That dataset gives Maurice Byer 121.6 km², where its own published clinical catchment sums to roughly 150 km² by parish area. Ask the Ministry whether one boundary set governs both before it is used to tell a citizen where to go for care.
      • The split inside St. Michael is traced by hand. Four polyclinics sit in that parish and no published source subdivides it, so the three sub-areas shown (Eunice Gibson north, Branford Taitt north-west, Winston Scott south and east) are an informed guess, not a boundary.
      • Shared parishes are assigned to one polyclinic. St. James is split north/south between Maurice Byer and Eunice Gibson, part of St. Thomas goes to Maurice Byer, and part of St. Philip to Randal Phillips — but no source says where those lines fall, so the whole parish takes its primary polyclinic's colour and a dashed edge.
      • Map pin positions are placed by hand, not geocoded. Every facility is tagged Approximate.
      • Opening hours disagree across official sources. health.gov.bb says all polyclinics run 08:30–16:30 with Winston Scott 24/7; the GIS says Winston Scott and David Thompson are both 24/7; the polyclinics' own websites give a third set of times. Both readings are shown where they conflict.
      • Phone numbers disagree. The polyclinic websites carry pre-migration numbers; the 536-xxxx numbers from health.gov.bb are shown here as the newer set.
      • Five polyclinic websites carry the wrong catchment description — Branford Taitt, Edgar Cochrane, Glebe and St. Philip all describe Randal Phillips' catchment, and Eunice Gibson describes Maurice Byer's. Catchment text here does not come from those pages.
      • Edgar Cochrane Polyclinic has no published catchment area. It began as a satellite of Ladymeade (now Winston Scott). Confirm with the Ministry whether it has one.
      • David Thompson has no facility website, so its specialist clinic days are unknown.
      • Naming is unsettled: health.gov.bb calls it Frederick "Freddie" Miller Polyclinic; its own site and the Barbados Drug Service call it Glebe Polyclinic.
      • Two different design-system builds exist, and they disagree. This page uses the stylesheet the live alpha.gov.bb service pages use, vendored locally. The npm @govtech-bb/styles@alpha package is a different build: different class names (govbb-container, govbb-btn), a different token prefix (--color-* rather than --govbb-*), no bundled Tailwind, no responsive heading scale, and a broken skip link — its .govbb-visually-hidden-focusable is permanently visible when used alone and stays 1×1 even when focused when combined with .govbb-visually-hidden. The live build has a working .govbb-skip-link instead. Worth confirming with the design-system team which build is canonical.
      • Basemap tiles are served by Esri, a third-party commercial service, because the two key-free providers both now fail silently. See the comment in the page source; this needs a decision before beta.
