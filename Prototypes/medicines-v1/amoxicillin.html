Amoxicillin

Last updated on November 11th, 2026

Also sold as Amotid, Amoxapen or Amoxil.

At a glance

Is it free?

Yes, with a prescription from a doctor or dentist in Barbados, if you are a Barbadian citizen or permanent resident. You pay for some options.

Check the options available on the formulary, or ask your pharmacist.

Where can I get it?

To get it free: visit a polyclinic pharmacy.

For a small fee instead: a private pharmacy that works with the Barbados Drug Service, if you are 65 or over, under 16 or have one of the conditions in Group A below.

To buy it: visit a private pharmacy.

White prescriptions can only be taken to a private pharmacy. Yellow or green prescriptions (usually from the Queen Elizabeth Hospital) can be taken to the hospital pharmacy, or to some government pharmacies for selected medications. Check what the prescription colours mean.

Pharmacies sometimes run out of stock. Call the pharmacy before you go.

Find a pharmacy and check if it works with the Barbados Drug Service

Free or lower price

The options for amoxicillin are in Group A and Group C:

  • A

    Group A: free at a polyclinic pharmacy

    If you are 65 or over, under 16, or living with diabetes, high blood pressure, asthma, epilepsy, glaucoma or cancer, you can also get it at a private pharmacy that works with the Barbados Drug Service for a small fee (a dispensing fee).

    What Group A means

  • C

    Group C: you pay a lower price

    The price is lower because there is no duty or VAT on it.

    What Group C means

What to bring

If your medication is free, or you pay only the dispensing fee, bring your prescription and original ID for the person the medication is for (for a child, their ID card or passport). Photocopies are not accepted.

If you are paying for it, you only need your prescription.

What to bring, including for children, permanent residents and someone collecting for you

Options on the formulary

Your prescription or pharmacist will tell you which one you are given. They all contain amoxicillin.

These are the formulary's listed prices in Barbados dollars. If you get it free, you pay nothing. If you pay, the price at a pharmacy may be different.

Need help?

Contact the Barbados Drug Service if you have a problem getting your medication. For example, if:

  • your ID is not accepted
  • you are asked to pay more than you expected
  • the pharmacy does not have your medication
  • Telephone: (246) 535-4300
  • Email: management@drugservice.gov.bb
  • Address: 6th Floor, Warrens Tower II, Warrens, St. Michael.
