Glaucoma

Timolol eye drops

Last updated on October 28th, 2026

Timolol eye drops lower the pressure inside your eye if you have glaucoma.

Also called Timoptol or Timoptic. These are brand names for the same medication.

In an emergency

Call 511 for an ambulance if someone:

  • took too much of a medication
  • has chest pain or trouble breathing
  • has a swollen face

You can also go to Accident and Emergency at the Queen Elizabeth Hospital.

Getting your medication

Can I get it for free?

You might, if you are:

  • 65 or over
  • under 16
  • living with glaucoma

You must be a Barbadian citizen or permanent resident.

To check if you can get it free, call the Barbados Drug Service on (246) 535-4300 or email management@drugservice.gov.bb. Your pharmacist can also tell you.

Read more about free or cheaper medication

How much does it cost if I pay the full amount?

30 Barbados dollars for 1 bottle (5 ml).

If you cannot get it free, you may pay less than this at a pharmacy that works with the Drug Service.

Where can I get it?

At a pharmacy that works with the Barbados Drug Service. This includes polyclinic pharmacies.

At a private pharmacy, you may pay a fee to prepare your medication. This is called a dispensing fee.

What do I need to bring?

  • your prescription
  • original ID for the person the medication is for, such as a Barbados ID card or passport

Photocopies are not accepted.

If you are travelling, you can get up to 2 months' supply at a time.

About this medication

What does it treat?

Glaucoma. This is when pressure builds up in your eye and can damage your sight.

What does it look like?

It is a small bottle of eye drops.

Who makes it?

Northgate Laboratories

You may be given a different make. It works the same way.

What group is it in?

Group A

Any doctor or dentist can give you a prescription for it.

How do I take it?

Put 1 drop in each eye that needs it, twice a day. Keep using them even if your eyes feel fine.

Is it safe for everyone?

Not always. Tell your doctor if you have asthma or a slow heartbeat. The drops can affect your breathing and heart.

If you are not sure, ask your doctor or pharmacist.

Need help?

Contact the Drug Service if you have a problem getting your medication. For example, if:

  • your ID is not accepted
  • you are asked to pay more than you expected
  • the pharmacy does not have it
  • Telephone: (246) 535-4300
  • Email: management@drugservice.gov.bb
  • Address: 6th Floor, Warrens Tower II, Warrens, St. Michael
