Diabetes

Gliclazide

Last updated on October 21st, 2026

Gliclazide is a tablet that lowers the sugar in your blood if you have type 2 diabetes.

Also called Diamicron.

Is it free? Yes, for anyone with diabetes, at polyclinic pharmacies and at private pharmacies that work with the Drug Service. How to get your medication

About this medication

What does it treat?

Type 2 diabetes. It helps your body make more insulin, which brings your blood sugar down.

What does it look like?

They are white tablets. They come in 1 size: 80 mg.

Who makes it?

Island Generics Inc Example

You may be given a different make. It works the same way.

What group is it in?

Group A Example

Group A medications are free for many people. Any doctor or dentist can give you a prescription for them.

How do you take it?

Take it with breakfast, or with breakfast and your evening meal. Do not skip meals while you take it.

Is it safe for everyone?

Not always. It can make your blood sugar go too low, so you may feel shaky, sweaty or confused. Eat or drink something sugary and tell your doctor if this happens.

If you are not sure, ask your doctor or pharmacist.

In an emergency: if you feel very unwell after taking a medication, or you have taken too much, call 511 for an ambulance.

Source: Barbados National Drug Formulary. Checked on October 21st, 2026 Example

Getting your medication

Is it free for you?

At a polyclinic pharmacy, it is free if you are a Barbadian citizen or permanent resident. Not confirmed

At a private pharmacy that works with the Drug Service, it is free if one of these applies:

  • you are under 16
  • you are 65 or over
  • you need it for diabetes, high blood pressure, asthma, epilepsy, glaucoma or cancer

If none of these apply, you pay a lower price. There is no duty or VAT on it.

You need a prescription from a doctor or dentist.

Check who can get free or cheaper medication

Where can you get it?

  • At a polyclinic pharmacy, you do not pay to have it made up.
  • At a private pharmacy that works with the Drug Service, you pay a small fee to have it made up. This is called a dispensing fee.
  • At any other private pharmacy, you pay the full price.

What do you need to take?

  • your prescription
  • original ID for the person the medication is for, such as a Barbados ID card or passport

Photocopies are not accepted.

If you are travelling, you can get up to two months' supply at a time.

Contact the Drug Service if you have a problem getting your medication. For example, if your ID is not accepted, you are asked to pay more than you expected, or the pharmacy does not have it.
