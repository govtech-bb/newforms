Call 511 for an ambulance, or go to Accident and Emergency at the Queen Elizabeth Hospital.

If you can, tell someone you trust how you are feeling and ask them to stay with you.

Call 511 for an ambulance if someone:

  • took too much of a medication
  • has chest pain or trouble breathing
  • has a swollen face

You can also go to Accident and Emergency at the Queen Elizabeth Hospital.

If it is not an emergency, you can:

  • search for a medication by name
  • look through the A to Z of medications
  • call the Barbados Drug Service on (246) 535-4300

Before you go, call the Barbados Drug Service on (246) 535-4300 to check if you can collect medication for someone else, and what you need to bring.

To find out about their medication, search for it by name.

Some medications are free for children under 16, people 65 or over, and people living with diabetes, high blood pressure, asthma, epilepsy, glaucoma or cancer. You usually need to be a Barbadian citizen or permanent resident.

Search for your medication by name to see if it is free for you.

    Need help?

    Contact the Barbados Drug Service to check if your medication is free, or if you have a problem getting it. For example, if:

    • your ID is not accepted
    • you are asked to pay more than you expected
    • the pharmacy does not have it
    • Telephone: (246) 535-4300
    • Email: management@drugservice.gov.bb
    • Address: 6th Floor, Warrens Tower II, Warrens, St. Michael
